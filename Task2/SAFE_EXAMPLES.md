# Safe Phishing Examples

All examples are fictional training samples. They use reserved `.example` domains and are not live campaigns. Do not visit or interact with suspicious links in real messages.

## Example A — Urgent account warning

**Subject:** Immediate action required: account closes today  
**From:** `security-alert@account-check.example`  
**Message:** “Your account will be suspended in 30 minutes. Confirm your password and one-time code using the link below: `https://account-check.example/verify`.”

**Red flags**
- Artificial urgency and threat of account closure.
- Request for a password and one-time code.
- Sender/domain not independently verified.
- Link urges the reader to act from the message.

**Safe action:** Do not click or enter details. Open the service using its official app/bookmark or contact its support using known contact details.

## Example B — Unexpected invoice attachment

**Subject:** Overdue invoice — open immediately  
**From:** `billing@vendor-invoice.example`  
**Message:** “Payment is overdue. Open the unexpected attachment and enable macros to view the invoice.”

**Red flags**
- Unexpected invoice.
- Pressure to act immediately.
- Request to enable macros, which can execute risky content.

**Safe action:** Do not open the attachment or enable macros. Verify the invoice with the vendor using a known contact method and report the message.

## Example C — Gift card request

**Subject:** Quick favour, keep this confidential  
**From:** `manager-name@free-mail.example`  
**Message:** “I am in a meeting. Buy gift cards now and send me the codes. Do not call me.”

**Red flags**
- Unusual payment request.
- Secrecy and urgency.
- Sender identity may be spoofed or a lookalike.

**Safe action:** Verify through a separate trusted channel. Follow normal purchasing approval procedures and report suspicious requests.

## Quick red-flag checklist

- Is the sender address unexpected or slightly misspelled?
- Was the message expected and does the request fit normal work?
- Does it pressure you with urgency, fear, secrecy, or a reward?
- Does it ask for passwords, OTPs, payment, or sensitive data?
- Does the link's displayed text differ from the actual destination?
- Is an unexpected attachment asking you to enable macros or bypass protections?

One sign alone does not prove phishing. Verify context and report concerns.
