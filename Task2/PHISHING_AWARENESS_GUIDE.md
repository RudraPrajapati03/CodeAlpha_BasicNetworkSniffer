# Phishing Awareness Training

## Goal
Help learners recognize phishing attempts, avoid unsafe interactions, and report suspicious messages. This is a defensive awareness project; it does not send emails, collect credentials, or imitate a real login page.

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this short session, learners should be able to:
- Explain phishing and common variants (email, SMS/smishing, voice/vishing, QR-code phishing).
- Identify suspicious sender addresses, urgency, unexpected attachments, mismatched links, and requests for passwords or one-time codes.
- Recognize common fake-website clues without assuming any one clue proves fraud.
- Verify requests using a trusted channel rather than replying to the suspicious message.
- Report suspicious messages through their organization's approved process.

## 10-minute lesson plan
1. **Introduction (2 min):** Phishing is social engineering designed to trick people into revealing information, sending money, or opening unsafe content.
2. **Spot the signs (3 min):** Inspect the sender, greeting, context, request, link destination, attachments, spelling, urgency, and unusual payment/account requests.
3. **Fake websites (2 min):** Check the exact registered domain, unexpected login/payment prompts, unusual redirects, and requests for credentials/OTPs. HTTPS/ the padlock encrypts the connection but does not prove a site is legitimate.
4. **Practice (2 min):** Read the safe examples in [SAFE_EXAMPLES.md](./SAFE_EXAMPLES.md) and identify red flags.
5. **Knowledge check (1 min):** Complete [QUIZ.md](./QUIZ.md), then review the answer key.

## Fake website safety checklist
- Watch for lookalike or misspelled domains, extra words, and misleading subdomains. Read the registered domain carefully.
- Treat unexpected password, OTP, payment, or account-recovery prompts as suspicious.
- Unexpected redirects, pressure tactics, poor context, or requests to bypass browser/security warnings are warning signs.
- A professional-looking page and HTTPS do not guarantee legitimacy.
- Do not open the link from the suspicious message. Use the official app, a known bookmark, or an address you type yourself; verify through independently sourced contact details.

## What to do with a suspicious message
1. Do not click links, open unexpected attachments, reply, or share passwords/OTPs.
2. Verify the request through a known, independent channel (for example, the official app or a phone number you already trust).
3. Report it using your organization's phishing-report button or IT/security contact.
4. If you clicked or entered information, report it promptly. Change affected credentials through the official site, revoke active sessions if supported, and follow your IT team's instructions.
5. Preserve the message for investigation; do not forward it broadly.

## Safety and evaluation
Use only the fictional, defanged examples in this repository. Do not send simulated phishing to real people without explicit organizational authorization and safeguards. No real credentials or personal data should be collected.

Suggested evaluation: record the number of quiz answers correct out of five, discuss the missed concepts, and repeat the quiz after the lesson. Do not use quiz results to shame participants.
